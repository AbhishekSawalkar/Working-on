Practically: TCS will not spike as much as Nifty in bull markets, but also won't crash as hard. The current -30% decline is an anomaly driven by sector-specific headwinds, not general market weakness.

### S&P 500 Correlation & Lag Effect
TCS has **near-zero correlation** with the S&P 500 (r = 0.17) — much lower than expected for an IT company with 53% US revenue. This seems counterintuitive but can be explained by: (1) S&P 500 and Indian market timing differences, (2) FII flows acting as the transmission mechanism with a delay.

**Lag Effect (US → India Next Day):**
> When S&P 500 moves significantly on day T (US close), TCS shows a **0.16 correlation** on day T+1 (when Indian market opens the next morning).

This lag effect is **weak for TCS specifically** (r = 0.16 vs typical 0.25–0.40 for broader Nifty). Why? TCS's price driver is **company-specific** (quarterly results, deal wins, guidance) rather than macro sentiment. S&P 500 moves matter to TCS mainly when they signal changes in US corporate IT spending appetite.

**Practical implication for swing traders:** Monitor US tech earnings (Microsoft, Accenture) the night before — a strong US tech rally could give TCS a 0.5–1% tailwind at open, but it won't sustain without domestic fundamental support.

### Deep Dive: Key Historical Events

#### 2018 — The Great Re-Rating (+73% in 6 months)
- **Catalyst:** TCS reported Q4 FY18 revenue of $4.98B — first time crossing $5B annualized run rate. Operating margin hit 25.6%. Simultaneously announced a ₹16,000 Cr share buyback (one of India's largest).
- **Technical setup:** Stock had been consolidating at ₹1,000–₹1,100 for 2 years (2015–2017). Breakout from this base with strong volume.
- **Macro context:** USD/INR at 68+ (favorable for IT exports); US corporate tax cut under Trump boosted US client IT budgets.
- **Pattern:** 2-year consolidation base → earnings breakout → re-rating. Classic "cup and handle" formation.

#### March 2020 — COVID Crash and Spectacular Recovery
- **Crash cause:** TCS fell 40% in 6 weeks (₹2,366 → ₹1,422) on fear that COVID would freeze IT spending.
- **Recovery cause:** Exact opposite happened — COVID massively accelerated IT spending. Cloud migration, digital banking, e-commerce, remote work all created unprecedented demand for TCS services.
- **Speed of recovery:** TCS recovered to pre-COVID levels by September 2020 (6 months) — much faster than broader market.
- **Pattern:** V-shaped recovery driven by fundamental realization. Panic sellers at ₹1,422 were early — the stock went to ₹4,312 eventually.
- **Key lesson:** In quality stocks like TCS, panic sell-offs driven by macro fear (not fundamental deterioration) are buying opportunities.

#### 2022 — Fed Rate Hike Impact
- **Cause:** US Fed raised rates by 425 bps in 2022. This caused: (1) US tech sector repricing → reduced IT budgets; (2) INR appreciated briefly → TCS USD earnings worth less in INR; (3) wage inflation surged 15–20% as talent was scarce post-COVID.
- **TCS-specific:** Attrition hit 22%+ (record high). Subcontracting costs spiked. Margins compressed by ~200 bps.
- **Pattern:** Rate-hike-driven IT corrections are cyclical. They typically last 6–12 months as margins stabilize.

#### Sep 2024 – Mar 2026 — Current Decline (The Bear Phase)
- **Phase 1 (Sep–Dec 2024):** TCS peaked at ₹4,312. Soft Q2 FY25 results — revenue growth slowed to 4.9%. Market started pricing in IT slowdown.
- **Phase 2 (Jan–Mar 2025):** FII selling intensified. Nifty IT as a whole fell. TCS compounded by sector-wide de-rating.
- **Phase 3 (Oct 2025–Mar 2026):** Announcement of 12,000 layoffs (March 2026) — first mass layoff in TCS history. $100B market cap lost since 2021. Stock hit 5.5-year low.
- **Current status:** TCS is at levels last seen October 2020, having given up all post-COVID gains.
